# Divergences register: INTRO and COVERT

The set-up screen's and the launcher's part of the register (DIVERGENCES.md says what an entry is). Ids D-600 and up.

| ID | Where | The original | The C# | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-600 | `HardwareSetup.ShowTitle`, `HardwareSetup.HardwareSetupMenu` | First asks "Select graphics mode:" with five cards (VGA, MCGA, EGA, Tandy 1000, CGA); the digit key chooses the graphics driver's letter (E, M, E, T, C) for the hand-off block, which the launcher turns into the driver it loads and the parts into the names of the pictures they load and into their colours (CGA's four). | The question is not shown: the player's game shows only the set-up screen's title (`ShowTitle`, D-603), and the letter is MCGA's (the second of the program's own table, "2) MCGA (256 color)"), as if the player had chosen it. The original's questions are kept for the proof (`HardwareSetupMenu`, run with `GameOptions.AskSetUpQuestions`): there the graphics question is printed as in the original (blank rows, the prompt, the five menu lines, the cursor at the answer) but not asked, no key is read, and the sound and controls questions are asked as in the original. Evidence (whole-session proof, item 7 of SESSION-PROOF.md): the original's page at the sound question keeps the graphics prompt's yellow on row 13 and on the graphics menu's cells the sound menu does not cover (87 cells); the rewrite, which printed nothing for the question, had them grey until it printed the question too (`HardwareSetupTests.TheGraphicsQuestionLeavesItsColoursUnderTheSoundQuestion`). The session proof answers the original's question with the spec's `graphics_key` ("2"), outside the key script. | The rewrite has one graphics driver, the 256-colour MCGA driver (DECISIONS.md); any other answer would make the parts load pictures and colours for a driver that is not there. The question is not shown (the user's decision of 15 November 2026, RELEASE-1.0.md) because the other answers the original offers on a VGA show the same picture: across all 95 recorded sessions the game set 13 distinct palettes and only ever used colour numbers 0..15, which MGRAPHIC's table maps to the standard 16 RGBI colours that EGRAPHIC's palette registers give in its 200-line mode (`(c & 0x0f) + 8 & 0x17`, docs/vga-check.md). So MCGA, EGA, the "VGA" choice (which loads EGRAPHIC, CA-48) and Tandy show the same picture; no program tells E from M, and only C (CGA, four colours) differs, which is not offered. |
| D-602 | `Game.Main` | Points the BIOS's spare far pointer (0000:04F0) at the hand-off block; runs INTRO.EXE with `spawnl`; loads the graphics driver (?GRAPHIC.EXE with FONTS.CV), MISC.EXE and the sound driver (?SOUND.CA) as overlays and starts them (their segments go into the hand-off block); runs each program with `spawnl`; at the end stops the drivers and sets text mode 3. | The set-up and the parts are method calls on the same hand-off object; the drivers are the objects the game was made with; nothing is done at the end (the host closes its window). The loop on the "next part" code, its first code (0x0b) and the clearing of Num Lock before each part are kept; the loop also ends when the host's window is closed. Seen in the whole-session proof (item 7 of SESSION-PROOF.md): the block's six bytes SHARED+0x60..0x65 hold the original's driver segments from the first part on and are 0 in the rewrite, and so are the same six bytes of a saved game (CV?.SVE offset 0x50..0x55, the block's head from SHARED+0x10), which neither side reads back (`CampaignSaves.ReadHeader` keeps the session's own); the C library's spawn asks DOS whether a key is waiting before each EXEC (COVERT 0000:0EBB, INT 21h AH=0Bh), which the rewrite has no counterpart of (a key stays in the buffer for the part either way); the text mode set at the end comes after the last key. | One C# program has no programs to spawn and no driver programs to load (ARCHITECTURE.md "No emulator", D-2); what a part sees (the hand-off block, the drivers' services) is the same. A closed window already ends every part (`ProgramExitException`); the loop must not start the next one. |
| D-603 | `Game.Main`, `HardwareSetup.ShowTitle` (the launcher's settings, `GameOptions`) | COVERT.EXE runs INTRO.EXE at every start: the set-up screen asks for the graphics card (D-600), the sound card and the controls, and its answers go into the hand-off block; it ends with "One Moment Please..." while the launcher loads the drivers. | The set-up screen shows its framed title (the version and the copyright) and "One Moment Please..." in its place and colour, as the original leaves the page after the last answer, for two seconds (140 frames of the 70 Hz display), and reads no key. The answers come from the launcher's settings: the MCGA driver's letter (D-600), the sound card's letter (`sound.card` or `--sound`: none, speaker, tandy, adlib, mt32; AdLib by default) and the joystick flag (`input.controls` or `--controls`: "joystick", the default, is the first answer "1. Joystick and Keyboard", 1, when a joystick or gamepad is found by the end of the title, else 0; "keyboard" the second, 0; the menus part then calibrates the joystick as after the question). The original's questions are kept and proven (`HardwareSetupMenu`, `IntroOracleTests`, `HardwareSetupTests`); the whole-session proof runs them with the recordings' keys (`GameOptions.AskSetUpQuestions`, the session harness's `RewriteSide.RunWhole`). | The user's decision of 15 November 2026 for 1.0 (RELEASE-1.0.md): the graphics question has one answer (D-600), and the sound card and the controls are asked once, in the launcher, instead of at every start. The launcher's earlier "As chosen at the set-up screen" and "Skip the set-up screen" are gone; a settings file with them still loads (the card as AdLib, the skip ignored). |
