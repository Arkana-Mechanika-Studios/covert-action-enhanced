# Uncertain register

Every place where the C# rests on a **guess**: a field whose meaning is inferred, a behaviour taken from one
kind of machine, a reading of assembly not yet confirmed by a run. Each entry has a `// UNCERTAIN: U-n ...`
comment at the place in the code; `TraceabilityTests` fails when a marker has no entry here or an entry has no
marker. An entry leaves the register when an oracle vector, a run of the original or the driver's code settles it
(say how in the commit); if the guess was wrong the code changes with it.

|---|---|---|---|
| U-6 | `Audio.Synthesis.SpeakerVoice` | How the PC speaker sounds: the square wave of the timer's data sheet averaged per frame, a fixed level and a 20 Hz high-pass for the cone. The driver's divisor and switch sequence is proven; the sound made of it is a model. | A recording of a real PC playing the same commands, compared with the rendered frames. |
| U-7 | `Audio.Synthesis.OplSynth` | How the AdLib card sounds: the Darklands project's OPL3 model in its OPL2 mode, taken from the chip's 49,716 frames a second to the device's rate by linear interpolation, at a fixed gain. | U-8 | `Audio.Synthesis.MidiPort` | How the MT-32 sounds: Munt's mt32emu (through `Audio.Mt32.Mt32Synth`, reused from the Darklands project) with the player's MT-32 or CM-32L ROMs, rendered at the device's rate. The driver's MIDI and system-exclusive bytes are proven; the sound made of them is the emulation's. | A recording of a real MT-32 playing the same commands, compared with the rendered frames. |
| U-9 | `Audio.Roland.RolandSoundDriver` (the voices at DS:2da2, the held notes at DS:2eb4) | The driver's variables behind the end of RSOUND.CA's data are 0 until the driver sets them, as in the oracle's fixture: so a voice's first rest ends four held notes 0 of its channel, and a resume before a voice ever played gives its channel volume 0. Nothing of the game clears them: the launcher's load_overlay (COVERT 011a:034c) takes the largest free block (DOS 48h), loads the driver into it with EXEC 4B03h, which writes the file's image only, and shrinks the block (4Ah), and the driver sets them only as it plays. They hold what that memory held before. In the whole sessions they are 0 (practice-return-roland: no instruction of INTRO, the launcher or the earlier drivers writes there, and the session machine's memory starts at 0), so the sessions cannot tell. | Not the game's code any more but the PC's memory: a dump of a real PC's memory at the driver's segment +0x4522..+0x465c right after the launcher has loaded RSOUND.CA (it depends on DOS, the resident programs and what ran before the game). |
| U-10 | `Audio.Synthesis.Sn76496Voice` | How the Tandy's chip sounds: the SN76496's counters followed at the clock divided by 16 (3,579,545 Hz) and averaged per frame, 2 dB per attenuation step at a fixed level, a 15-bit noise register with feedback from bits 0 and 1 (white) or bit 0 (periodic), a period of 0 taken as 1024. The driver's byte sequence is proven; the sound made of it is a model. | A recording of a real Tandy 1000 or PCjr playing the same commands, compared with the rendered frames; the noise register's width and taps from a chip's data sheet or a recording. |
| U-11 | `Audio.Tandy.TandySoundDriver.Random` | The driver's random number adds the carry flag as the interrupted program left it; the C# and the vectors have it clear. The driver's code shows it is exactly the interrupted instruction's carry: the timer handler (TSOUND 0000:096d) pushes, loads DS and calls the tick (0131), whose `inc` of the tick count leaves the carry alone, and next_random (0285) begins with `mov` and `adc`. The engine's, the gunfire's and the tunes' random pitches depend on it. Seen in the whole-session proof (SESSION-PROOF.md item 8): with the interrupted program's carry at each tick (set at 19 of 180 ticks of practice-return-tandy, from checkpoint 2), the original's stream left the rewrite's at checkpoint 97 (CODE's effect 45: tone channel 2's period byte C0h against CAh); the session's machine now delivers its ticks with the carry clear, as the vectors do, and the Tandy sessions agree. | No reading can: the carry is that of whichever instruction a tick interrupts, which depends on the PC's speed and the moment. A real Tandy's recording with the same commands would show how often it is set there (which of the game's wait loops a tick usually interrupts, and their flags). |
| U-12 | `Platform.JoystickReading.FromStick` | The counts a real analog joystick gives MISC.EXE's timing loop: the game port's timer taken from the IBM game control adapter's description (24.2 microseconds plus 0.011 per ohm, a stick of 0 to 100 kilohms) and one turn of the loop as 45 cycles of an 8086 at 4.77 MHz (3 at one end, 61 in the middle, 119 at the other). The driver's calibration and scaling of the counts are proven (MiscJoystickOracleTests); the counts themselves are a model, and on a faster PC they are larger. Only the rounding of the game's axis bytes depends on them, since the driver scales every reading against the centre and the extremes it has seen. The whole session `joystick-menu` (SESSION-PROOF.md, item 12) feeds the model's counts to the original's MISC.EXE, run whole, and to the rewrite: the menus move alike at every checkpoint. That proves the rewrite's use of the counts, not the model: the menus only ask whether an axis byte is beyond 0x60 of the centre, which any counts with the stick at rest in the middle and pushed to an end give. Tried by hand with a real gamepad through SDL on 16 November 2026: the calibration, the menus and a raid play as expected; that tries the host's mapping, not a real PC's counts. | A real PC's counts with a real joystick (a run of MISC.EXE's loop on a 4.77 MHz PC), or the adapter's schematic and the 558 timer's capacitor. |
